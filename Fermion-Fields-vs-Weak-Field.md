## Question: Why Weak Force Field isn't involved in fermion creation (ie interactions with fermions as outputs), even if fermions always carry weak-isospin?

As per notes:

1. "There are 12 distinct matter fields: 6 distinct quark fields, 6 distinct lepton fields.
- Each matter particle (eg Up-quark, Down-quark, Tauon, Tau-neutrino etc) forms from its own parent matter field."

2. "For SU(2) (Weak Force Field), force-carriers are of 3 types (W+, W−, Z0), and matter has 2 flavor (weak-isospin) states: (+1/2 aka Up-type aka Up-flavor, −1/2 aka Down-type aka Down-flavor). Weak-isospin states are always present for both Quarks and Leptons, and  weak-isospin determines the identity of the Quark/Lepton."

Question:
So how come Weak Force Field wasn't involved in the interaction that created a matter particle (fermion), and only its own parent fermion field was involved?

## Answer: Weak-isospin is also a Quantum Number. A fermion field (represented by a Dirac Spinor Field) has chiral components ($\psi_L$ and $\psi_R$) that determine a fermion's weak-isospin Quantum Number

In the Standard Model of particle physics, the weak isospin ($T$ or $I$) of fermions is not just an arbitrary label; 
it is fundamentally hardwired into the structure of the **Electroweak Lagrangian density** via **gauge symmetry ($SU(2)_L$)** and **chiral projections**.

The confusion here comes down to a fundamental distinction in Quantum Field Theory (QFT): the difference between **existing as an independent quantum field** 
and **interacting via a gauge force**.

Here is why a matter particle (fermion) is created by its own parent fermion field without any role of the Weak Force Field, even though that fermion possesses weak-isospin.

---

### 1. Fields vs. Interactions in the Lagrangian

In the Standard Model, the mathematical description of the universe (the Lagrangian) is split into different pieces:

* **The Fermion Kinetic & Mass Terms:** Every fermion (each of the 12 matter fields, like the electron field or up-quark field) has its own independent term.
  These terms dictate that the field exists, propagates through spacetime, and can be excited into particles independently of any force fields.
* **The Gauge Interaction Terms:** The Weak Force Field ($SU(2)_L$ gauge bosons: $W^\pm, Z^0$) enters the equations through **coupling terms** (interaction vertices).
  These terms describe how existing left-handed fermions *interact* with or scatter off the $W$ and $Z$ bosons.

A force field does not "spawn" the matter field; rather, the matter fields exist fundamentally, and the force fields describe the rules by which certain matter fields talk to each other.

### 2. How Particles Are Created (Creation Operators)

In QFT, creating a specific particle (like an electron or a down-quark) means applying a **creation operator** ($\psi^\dagger$) associated with 
that specific matter field to the vacuum state.

* To create an electron, you excite the **electron field**.
* To create a down-quark, you excite the **down-quark field**.

The weak force bosons ($W^\pm, Z^0$) are themselves excitations of the weak force fields. While a weak interaction process (like beta decay) can *transform* one fermion 
into another (e.g., a down-quark turning into an up-quark by emitting a $W^-$ boson), the baseline existence and creation of the fermions themselves stem from their 
own respective field operators, not from the creation of a $W$ or $Z$ boson.

### 3. What Weak-Isospin Actually Does

Your notes correctly state that weak-isospin determines the identity/behavior of quarks and leptons under the weak force. However, **weak-isospin is a quantum number 
describing a particle's charge under the $SU(2)$ symmetry group**—similar to how electric charge ($Q$) is a quantum number describing a particle's coupling to 
the electromagnetic field (photons).

* Having an electric charge means a particle *interacts* with photons, but photons aren't required to "create" or "own" electrons.
* Similarly, having a weak-isospin state (+1/2 or -1/2) means a left-handed fermion *couples* to $W$ and $Z$ bosons.
  It dictates **how** the particle reacts to the weak force, but it does not mean the weak force field generated or birthed the fermion field in the first place.

---

### Summary

Think of the 12 matter fields as **independent musical instruments**, and the Weak Force Field as a **harmony rule** that dictates how certain instruments can duet or morph into one another (e.g., transforming a neutrino into an electron). An instrument can be played (a particle created) purely through its own strings, without needing the harmony rule.
